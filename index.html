I started with pixels.
Now I think in systems.

Frontend development taught me to obsess over the experience , every millisecond, every interaction, every edge case a real user would hit. But I kept running into the same ceiling: great interfaces on fragile infrastructure. So I went deeper. Cloud architecture, distributed systems, Kubernetes, infrastructure as code. My foundation in electronics and telecommunications gave me an early instinct for systems thinking: how things connect, fail, and recover. I wanted to own the full stack of what I build , and understand what makes it break at scale.

Now I am pushing into Applied AI. Not because it is trendy, but because it is the most interesting engineering problem I have encountered: making systems that reason, that learn, that automate the repetitive so humans can focus on what matters. If something can be automated, I am already thinking about how.

What drives me is building things that grow, scale, and hold up under pressure. I think about the future of a product before I write the first line. I am not afraid of new territory , the AI space moves fast, and I move with it.
